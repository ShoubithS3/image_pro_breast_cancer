MAMMO LENS Review 1 Proposal

Mammogram classification with global and local evidence

CAI3400 Image Processing and Analysis
Presidency University Bengaluru
Review date 5 November 2026

This proposal presents the research question, primary literature, audited dataset and preliminary real-data results. The next phase evaluates deep global/local models and probability reliability across density categories and controlled degradation.

Project proposal and objectives

MAMMO-LENS investigates retrospective benign versus malignant classification of selected abnormal mammograms. The proposed experiment asks whether global context and local patches improve performance, and whether any benefit remains reliable across recorded density categories and controlled image degradation.

The project combines a reproducible local image-analysis pipeline with a browser evidence viewer. Its objectives are to compare global, patch-only and combined features on identical patient partitions; evaluate probability quality and selective prediction; and examine density subgroup and degradation failures. Future cancer-risk prediction requires longitudinal outcomes, which this experiment does not contain.

Research hypothesis

Deep global/local fusion may improve classification over a global-only reference. Density conditioning may improve reliability in some groups. Both are hypotheses to test, rather than established advantages. Existing GMIC work already combines global and local information [6], so the proposed contribution is the controlled reliability experiment and transparent cohort audit.

Scope

The primary endpoint is image-level pathology classification. An exploratory patient endpoint takes the maximum image score and labels a patient positive if any included image is malignant. This selected abnormal-case cohort cannot establish general screening prevalence, population predictive value or clinical readiness.

Literature review

[1] Lee et al. (2017)

A curated mammography data set for use in computer-aided detection and diagnosis research

Curated DDSM cases with pathology, mass/calcification annotations and breast-density descriptors. Defines the dataset and label provenance. Our audited Kaggle JPEG subset differs from the original DICOM collection. Selected abnormal cases support retrospective classification. Future outcomes and screening prevalence require other data.

[2] He et al. (2016)

Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition

Residual connections ease optimization of deep image networks. The paper evaluates natural-image recognition. Provides the ResNet family used for our planned ImageNet-initialized ResNet18 encoder. Natural-image benchmark success does not establish mammography performance.

[3] Ilse et al. (2018)

Attention-based Deep Multiple Instance Learning

Learns a permutation-invariant attention operator for bags of instances, with experiments including histopathology. Provides gated attention for pooling selected patch embeddings. Different tissue/task and data. Attention weights do not establish causal or clinical explanations.

[4] Guo et al. (2017)

On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks

Studies probability calibration and evaluates temperature scaling on image and document classification. Motivates fitting one temperature using validation predictions and evaluating probability quality separately. Calibration must be re-evaluated under dataset or image-quality shift.

[5] Li Shen et al. (2019)

Deep Learning to Improve Breast Cancer Early Detection on Screening Mammography

Uses annotated patches to initialize an all-convolutional whole-image classifier, with DDSM development and INbreast transfer. Establishes prior patch-to-image mammography learning and motivates a controlled local/global comparison. Its annotations, cohort and transfer protocol differ from our experiment. Headline scores are not comparable.

[6] Yiqiu Shen et al. (2021)

An interpretable classifier for high-resolution breast cancer screening images utilizing weakly supervised localization

GMIC combines a lightweight global network, saliency-selected high-resolution patches, gated attention and fusion on NYU screening mammograms. Closest architectural precedent. It motivates retaining local detail and explicitly crediting existing fusion and attention techniques. Our current patches come from a 256-square image and use texture selection. We cannot claim equivalent localization or clinical validation.

The reviewed sources establish the component methods and close mammography precedents. They do not establish that this project's exact combination is novel. Additional recent and density-specific literature remains part of the continuing review. Cross-paper AUROC comparisons are inappropriate without matched cohorts, supervision and split protocols.

Methodology and evaluation

Each image is converted to grayscale while preserving intensity range, normalized between its first and 99.5th intensity percentiles and resized to 256 by 256 pixels. Four 64 by 64 grid patches with high intensity variation are selected after background filtering. Texture selection does not locate lesions.

The completed handcrafted models use intensity histograms, intensity statistics, quantiles and gradient magnitudes. Global mode uses the whole image. Patch mode pools patch feature means and maxima. Combined mode concatenates both. A StandardScaler and logistic regression fit only the training features.

The implemented deep model uses a shared ImageNet-initialized ResNet18 [2]. It encodes a 224-square global view and four 128-square resized patches. Gated attention [3] pools patch embeddings. The classification head consumes global, local or concatenated features. A supervised density head uses known density labels. Optional density-conditioned fusion consumes predicted density probabilities during inference. Real deep checkpoints and scores remain pending.

For the planned initial deep comparison, use a frozen encoder, batch size 8, seed 42 and 10 epochs, holding budget and selection rule constant. Validation log loss selects the checkpoint. Validation-only temperature scaling [4] calibrates scores. Deep tuning, higher-resolution alternatives and runtime optimization remain future work.

Report AUROC, sensitivity and specificity at a stated threshold, Brier score, density subgroup counts and coverage versus error. Current intervals resample patients with all their views in 200 cluster bootstrap replicates. Evaluate blur radius 2 and Gaussian noise standard deviation 0.05 as paired perturbations at working resolution. Patch masking replaces a selected region with median intensity and recomputes the pipeline, so score changes cannot isolate causal importance.

The global reference has the strongest point estimate. Combined features have not demonstrated an improvement, and overlapping intervals alone do not constitute a paired significance test. Patch-only features perform worse by point estimate. We will preserve this negative finding rather than assume fusion must help.

Reliability observations

For the global baseline, recorded density category 4 has AUROC 0.4895 on 113 test images, compared with 0.6964 for category 2 on 258 images. Small groups and cohort composition limit interpretation. The result does not demonstrate a causal density effect.

Global AUROC under blur is 0.6197. Added noise reduces AUROC to 0.5902 and raises Brier score from 0.2359 to 0.3969. At threshold 0.5, noise produces sensitivity 0 and specificity 1 because all outputs fall on the benign side. This highlights a severe perturbation failure.

For global predictions, confidence 0.6 retains 202 of 637 images with error 0.3020, versus error 0.3862 at full coverage. Confidence 0.8 retains only 8 images with error 0.75. Therefore increased confidence is not a dependable abstention rule here. Operational thresholds must be chosen on validation data.

The baseline test results have already been examined. Future changes motivated by these observations must be described as exploratory. Strong claims about generalization need a fresh external cohort or a prospectively specified evaluation.

Limitations and responsible interpretation

Major limitations are aggressive resizing, heuristic patch selection, one retrospective dataset, restricted abnormal-case sampling and incomplete external validation. Scanner robustness, clinical diagnosis and longitudinal risk remain outside demonstrated capabilities. All interfaces and presentations should retain research-only labeling.
